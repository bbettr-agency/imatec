## VISUAL SYSTEM (light-first)

**The website is predominantly clean, bright and light.** Engineering character comes from photography, precision and typography — not heavy industrial styling.

- **Backgrounds:** white `#FFFFFF` + soft off-white `#F6F8FA` dominant. Light steel greys (`#E4E9EF` / hairline `#CBD3DC`) for borders, dividers and subtle section differentiation.
- **Accent:** IMATEC Blue `#0B82E6` — controlled, reserved for primary CTAs and key marks. Accessible tints (`#0A6AC0` CTA, `#0A5FA8` links) carry white-on-blue and small text.
- **Typography:** charcoal `#343434`. **The only "dark" on the page is the authentic black recoated roller itself** — a product object, not a section. **No mandatory dark section** (removed).
- **Typeface: Open Sans throughout** — H1–H6, body, nav, buttons, forms, labels, mobile UI. Hierarchy via weight (800/700/600/400), size and spacing. **No industrial/condensed/futuristic/monospace type.** Small technical labels use Open Sans **uppercase + tracking + weight**, not monospace.
- **THE BENCH, expressed lightly:** authentic photography · close-up product/roller/component detail · workshop imagery · precise alignment · subtle thin technical lines · small spec/part details · structured, asymmetric layouts · excellent whitespace · strong Open Sans hierarchy · controlled blue accents · refined precise motion. Think *clean specialist technology company with real workshop capability*, not heavy industrial engineering.
- **Anti-generic guardrails:** no repeated heading+paragraph+3-cards, no endless white rounded cards, no blue gradient sections, no excessive pills, no glassmorphism, no floating blobs, no generic SaaS layouts. Each major section changes composition to suit its message.

## B. HOMEPAGE WIREFRAME STRATEGY (section sequence, nav → footer)

Rhythm is deliberate: **dense technical → breathing room → product evidence → process → human/workshop → conversion.** No two adjacent sections share composition; not one "heading + paragraph + three cards" section.

**01 · Navigation** — *Purpose:* orient + always-available contact. *Message:* the four worlds are discoverable instantly. *Visual:* transparent → solid-on-scroll; logo left; Recoating / Parts / Vacu-Tec / Mr McKenic / About / Contact; WhatsApp button right. *Conversion:* persistent WhatsApp. *SEO:* crawlable internal links to every key page. *Why:* wayfinding; signals breadth without a wall of text.

**02 · Hero — Capability / THE BENCH** — *Purpose:* answer the Blocking Question ("have I found the specialist, not a reseller?"). *Message:* recoat your fuser roller before you replace it — from a 30-year specialist workshop. *Visual:* asymmetric copy block + authentic recoated-roller as a labelled precision object (part-number chip, tolerance line) on generous neutral space. *Conversion:* primary "Enquire About Recoating" + secondary "See how recoating works". *SEO:* the one H1 (entity + category), first 100 words carry brand+category+location. *Why:* the differentiator is the hero; layout communicates competence. (Full spec in C.)

**03 · The Four Worlds** *(not four identical cards)* — *Purpose:* show breadth while ranking the four **different commercial relationships**. *Message:* one workshop, four capabilities — at their true weights. *Visual:* editorial asymmetric composition, each tagged by relationship type:
- **SPECIALIST SERVICE** — Fuser & Hot Roller Recoating *(dominant panel, bench image)*
- **IMATEC-BUILT PRODUCT** — Vacu-Tec *(medium, product shot)*
- **TRADE SUPPLY** — Printer/Copier Parts, Toner & Consumables *(wide band, brand names)*
- **SUPPLIED BRAND** — Mr McKenic *(smaller, quarantined styling)*
*Conversion:* each routes to its page + context CTA. *SEO:* internal links + entity clarity for all four. *Why:* directly implements the requested hierarchy; kills the "generic four-card grid".

**04 · Recoat vs Replace** *(the commercial wedge)* — *Purpose:* intercept the "I need a new roller" mindset. *Message:* before replacing, recoat — lower cost, faster than importing, keeps your OEM core. *Visual:* editorial two-state comparison (Replace: ~R2,000–R6,000 🟡 + import lead time + e-waste │ Recoat: restored to spec, faster turnaround³, keep the core) anchored by a worn→recoated roller. *Conversion:* "Enquire About Recoating". *SEO:* captures replacement-intent language on the homepage; feeds the flagship page. *Why:* this is the strategy's beating heart — uncontested messaging territory.

**05 · Why IMATEC** *(the vetting model — light feature band)* — *Purpose:* answer "why you / can I trust you / 30 years?". *Message:* we don't simply import — we test every part in our own workshop; we recoat what others replace; we build our own equipment. *Visual:* a **bright feature band** — a large authentic workshop photograph paired with three substantiated pillars on white / soft-steel, each claim → proof → so-what. **No dark section.** *Conversion:* soft (WhatsApp) + continue. *SEO:* trust/credibility entity signals. *Why:* the page's trust centre — carried by real imagery and specifics, not a heavy dark panel.

**06 · The Recoating Process** *(the moat)* — *Purpose:* prove recoating is real and rigorous. *Message:* strip → prep → recoat → cure → **bench-test** ⁴. *Visual:* a horizontal process sequence with a restrained connecting rule and authentic process imagery; **precise motion reveals steps in order** (motion explaining the system). *Conversion:* "See full process" → Recoating page. *SEO:* technical-authority content no competitor has → organic + AI citations. *Why:* competitors are catalogs; nobody explains the process — this is the authority moat.

**07 · Brand & Parts Coverage** — *Purpose:* prove supply breadth. *Message:* workshop-tested parts for 9 major brands. *Visual:* a clean brand-name band (typeset wordmarks, not logo soup) + parts categories + "Request a Part" entry. *Conversion:* "Request a Part". *SEO:* brand + component + model long-tail entry point. *Why:* proves they understand the whole workshop environment, not only recoating.

**08 · Workshop & Human Story** *(proof / breathing)* — *Purpose:* humanise 30 years; credibility. *Message:* the people and workshop behind the work. *Visual:* authentic workshop/technician imagery + (when gathered) a real testimonial/case-study slot — honest empty-state until then, **no fabricated proof**. *Conversion:* "About IMATEC". *SEO:* supports E-E-A-T. *Why:* balances the technical density with a human beat; sets up About.

**09 · Conversion + FAQ** — *Purpose:* convert + resolve top objections. *Message:* tell us what you need — we already understand it. *Visual:* **context-aware CTAs** (Enquire About Recoating / Request a Part / Enquire About Vacu-Tec / Enquire About Products) + send-your-roller reassurance + top objections as FAQ (FAQPage schema). *Conversion:* the primary conversion band. *SEO:* FAQ schema + intent coverage. *Why:* the page's close; intent-matched CTAs make IMATEC feel like it "gets" the enquiry before contact.

## C. HERO CREATIVE DIRECTION — "THE BENCH"

**The idea:** make the **recoated fuser roller the hero object** — a precise, matte-black cylinder photographed on IMATEC's own bench, presented with restrained *technical labelling* (a part-number chip, a tolerance/dimension line), on generous neutral space. The differentiator becomes the aesthetic. Clear, not cryptic.

### Copy spec (primary direction)

- **Eyebrow** (mono, wide tracking): `SPECIALIST FUSER & HOT ROLLER RECOATING`
- **H1:** **"Recoat your fuser roller — before you replace it."**
- **Supporting copy:** "For 30+ years¹, IMATEC has kept Africa's copiers and printers running — recoating fuser and hot rollers back to working spec, and supplying workshop-tested parts, consumables and our own service equipment to dealers and technicians nationwide."
- **Primary CTA:** `Enquire About Recoating` (WhatsApp-powered, pre-filled message)
- **Secondary CTA:** `See how recoating works` (→ process / Recoating page — a *different intent*, not a weaker synonym)
- **Reassurance micro** (mono): `Send us your roller² · No-obligation quote · We reply on WhatsApp`
- **Trust strip (3, verified/near-verified, beside the CTA):**
  1. `30+ years in office automation` ¹
  2. `9 brands — Ricoh · Canon · Konica Minolta · Kyocera · Xerox · HP · Sharp · Toshiba · Lexmark` ✅
  3. `Every part workshop-tested before we supply it` ✅
- **Roller object label** (mono, illustrative — real part refs from client): `FR · RECOATED TO SPEC` + a fine dimension tick.

**Alternative H1 (capability-led):** "The office-automation workshop that recoats what others replace." — hold as option; final wording after the truth gate.

### Answers the 5 required questions
1. **Who is IMATEC?** — logo + eyebrow + "30-year specialist workshop" (sub).
2. **What do they do?** — H1 (recoat rollers) + sub (recoat + supply + build).
3. **Why trust them?** — trust strip (tenure, 9 brands, workshop-tested) beside the CTA.
4. **Specialist capability?** — recoating is the H1 and the hero object.
5. **What next?** — one obvious primary CTA (Enquire About Recoating).

### Desktop composition (~1280×800)
Asymmetric, **not** a centred split. Left column (~55%): eyebrow → H1 (large precise grotesque, tight tracking) → supporting copy (≤65ch) → CTA pair → trust strip directly beneath. Right zone (~45%): the recoated roller as a horizontal cylindrical object on neutral/bone ground, with a part-number chip and a single fine tolerance/dimension line; a subtle baseline "measurement rule" runs along the hero's foot. One blue accent (primary CTA + part-number tick) only. Light, spacious — the dark roller is the focal contrast, not a dark section.

### Mobile composition (~390×844, ≤5 fold elements)
Logo + WhatsApp icon → eyebrow → H1 (≤3 lines) → full-width `Enquire About Recoating` → one condensed trust line (`30+ yrs · 9 brands · workshop-tested`). A tight **horizontal crop of the recoated roller** sits directly beneath as a slim evidence band (the cylinder reads well wide-and-short) with the part-number label — reinforcing immediately without pushing the CTA down. Sticky bottom bar: Call + WhatsApp.

### Motion behaviour (character: `precise, refined` — controlled, quiet polish, never heavy)
- **H1 = LCP → renders at first paint, never animated.**
- Secondary elements reveal via the `precise` heroStack ladder (hero ~0.6s, stagger 0.05), transform-only, total ≤900ms: supporting copy → CTA → trust strip.
- Roller image: slow scale-settle (scale only, never opacity; `priority` image never opacity-animated). The part-number chip snaps in a beat later — a small mechanical "measurement locks" moment.
- Optional: the baseline measurement rule draws once, left→right; must survive removal and reduced-motion.
- **Reduced motion:** everything static, instant, fully readable. No parallax on mobile.

### Why this hero is specifically IMATEC
No other office-automation site leads with a **recoated roller as a labelled precision object** and the **"recoat vs replace"** wedge only IMATEC can own in SA. The technical-labelling composition *is* the argument — it says "these people understand the equipment" in the layout itself, answering the Blocking Question before a word of body copy. It reuses none of the prior Bbettr heroes.

## APPENDIX 2 — Recoating Page: Minimum Client Questions (do not invent answers)
1. What exactly gets recoated? (fuser rollers, hot rollers, pressure rollers — which?)
2. What is the recoating process, step by step?
3. What problems does recoating solve? (marking, wear, print defects…)
4. When *can* a roller be recoated?
5. When can it **not** be recoated? (e.g. film-sleeve machines)
6. Which printer/copier brands are commonly supported?
7. Do you work from specific models or part numbers?
8. Typical turnaround time?
9. Warranty / guarantee?
10. How does someone send/deliver a roller?
11. Do you collect?
12. Nationwide courier process — how does it work, who pays?
13. Can customers send multiple rollers at once?
14. Who normally uses the service — dealers only, or end businesses too?
15. How does recoating compare with replacing the component (cost, time, life)?
16. What quality-control / testing happens before a roller is returned?
